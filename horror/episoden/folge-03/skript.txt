Mister Hundred takes you at eighteen if it finds you, and it called to me in my sister's voice.
Nobody can help you hide.
If they try, it takes them too.
My sister June hid me anyway, under her room.
"It never looks in my room, Rabbit."
Every night, she counts to one hundred before she falls asleep.
She says it helps.

I held my breath.
It still came out white.
Then the weight lifted off the boards.

Side by side is all it leaves.
It took her, I thought, because she helped me.

It stood up straight, taller than anyone knew.
Then it took its hands away from its face.
Her flashlight went out.

Then it came down Main Street, looking in every upstairs window.

Then it pulled back.

"Rabbit?"
"You can come out now."

I didn't answer.

"Ninety-nine."
"One hundred."
"Ready, Rabbit?"
I didn't answer.

It had June's face, far too big.

"It found me six years ago, Rabbit."
"Dad hid me down here, so it took him, and kept me."
"When I count, it sleeps."
"I thought it would never look under its own bed."
Then the huge hands shook, and June pulled them back over her own face.

"Count with me."

I counted without a sound.

Near dawn, her voice grew faint, like she was falling asleep.
At ninety-nine, she stopped.
Instead of one hundred, the sunrise bell rang.
When I opened my eyes, Hiding Night was over, and I was alone.

June's red sneakers still stood by her bed, side by side.
In the hall, my sneakers still stood side by side.
Every night since, I count to one hundred before I fall asleep.
